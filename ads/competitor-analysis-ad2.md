could not be opened from the build environment, so this reflects their public positioning, not

## Patterns: what they all do wrong

1. **They sell the technology.** "AI receptionist", "platform", "automation". Our owner isn't shopping for software. He has to recognise the problem first.
2. **They hide behind stats.** "391%", "62% of calls", "$78K a year". These are unsourced, recycled and ignored. A real scene beats a fake number.
3. **They put a robot or stranger between the owner and his customer.** The AI voice agents and call centres all do this. The owner's pride is that his customers deal with him.
4. **They make you switch systems.** Jobber, HCP and ServiceTitan only work on their platform. Others need a new number or app.
5. **They're generic.** US-centric, or every trade at once. Nobody says Calgary, a cold snap, or -28.
6. **They show dashboards and the vendor's brand,** not the owner's own phone.

### Why the lock-screen concept

Ad #1 showed what the homeowner sees. Ad #2 shows what the owner sees when he climbs out from
under the furnace: his own phone, -28 out, and two jobs waiting instead of two dead voicemails.
It's the realisation moment the brief asks for, in a picture he looks at 50 times a day.
