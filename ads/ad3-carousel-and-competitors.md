## 1. Who you're up against, and what they're doing

**A. AI receptionist companies** (Dapta, Newo, FirstDial, Central, Smith.ai, LeadTruffle)
- Pitch: "AI receptionist for HVAC that never sleeps, never takes lunch, never puts a customer on hold."
- Proof: big recycled stats ("home services miss up to 62% of calls", "30 to 50% more conversions").
- Pricing: published tiers (LeadTruffle $229 to $629 a month plus $299 onboarding).

**B. Field-service software add-ons** (Jobber Receptionist, Housecall Pro AI Team, ServiceTitan)
- Pitch: "never miss a customer interaction", answers calls and texts 24/7, books jobs.
- Catch: it only works if you run your whole business on their software.

**C. Answering services** (AnswerFirst, PATLive, Smith.ai live agents, After Hours Line)
- Pitch: live humans 24/7 for HVAC and plumbing. Industry pricing for live agents runs roughly $400 to $1,000+ a month.

**D. Small agencies and GoHighLevel resellers** (Pixorizon in Canada, MinuteLead, many US shops)
- Pitch: "Missed-call text back for HVAC, plumbing & mechanical", texts "within about 60 seconds", month-to-month.
- Content is mostly SEO blog posts with dollar math ("$78,000 a year lost").

**E. Local Calgary shops answering 24/7 themselves** (e.g. Gentleman Pros, Problem Solved Plumbing & Heating)
- Not selling to your buyer, but this is who your buyer loses the job to. "24/7, same day" is what homeowners pick.

## 2. What they're getting wrong

| Mistake | Why it fails with an owner-operator |
|---|---|
| Leading with "AI" | He's not shopping for software. "AI receptionist" makes him picture a robot talking to his customers. |
| Stat walls | "62%", "391%", "$78K". Unsourced, recycled, and every competitor says them. They read as marketing and get scrolled past. |
| Selling a replacement for him | AI voice and call centres replace the owner's voice. His reputation is that customers deal with him. |
| Lock-in | Jobber, HCP and ServiceTitan mean switching your whole system for one feature. |
| One-image, feature-list ads | A static list of features doesn't create the "oh, that happens to me" moment. Most of these ads get skipped in under a second. |
| Generic geography | US cities, or no place at all. Nothing says Calgary, a cold snap, or Okotoks. |
| Prices up front | Invites comparison shopping before he even understands the problem. |

## 3. What Ravara does better, and how Ad #3 uses it

1. **Tell the story instead of listing features.** The carousel walks him through the loss itself: missed call, then the homeowner calls the next company, then your line texts back, then it's nothing for him to do, then book. Every swipe tells Instagram the ad is interesting, which tends to lower your costs.
2. **Use the job value, not a statistic.** Slide 1 says "This missed call was a $9,000 furnace." That's the fear from your brief, not a claimed result.
3. **Show the homeowner's side.** Slide 2 shows the homeowner's recent calls: your business "No answer", next company "Answered · booked". Nobody else shows the job going to the competitor.
4. **Your name, not ours.** Slide 3 is a text thread from "Apex Heating & Air" in trade language ("No heat — got it.").
5. **Answer every competitor's weakness in one slide.** Slide 4: no app, no new number, no robot, no call centre, customers only see your name.
6. **Calgary everywhere.** The tag, Okotoks, the 403 number, and the town list on the last slide.
7. **No prices, one call to action.** Book a free call.

## 5. How to run it

- Put this carousel in the same ad set as your current two images, with equal budget, for 7 days.
- Judge on **cost per booked call**, not likes or clicks.
- Targeting: Calgary plus 40 km, ages 25 to 60, interests HVAC / plumbing / Jobber / Housecall Pro / ServiceTitan. Also try a broad audience; Meta often finds better buyers on its own.
- Run hardest ahead of cold snaps. That's when the fear is real.
- When you get your first client's 30-day numbers, replace slide 1 with that result.
